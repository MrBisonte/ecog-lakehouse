# ADR-0002: A PHI zone with pseudonymisation at the Silver boundary

| | |
|---|---|
| Status | proposed |
| Date | 2026-09-19 |
| Deciders | Alexandre Monteiro |
| Supersedes | none |

## Context

Clinical operations data identifies people and is needed with real identifiers by the clinical team. Recordings arrive from devices without identifiers and need a controlled join. GDPR Article 9 governs health data; ISO 14155 requires subject confidentiality with source data verification.

## Options considered

| Option | Summary | Rejected because |
|---|---|---|
| Pseudonymise at source, in the EDC | Identifiers never leave the clinical system | Clinical operations need real identifiers; recordings need a controlled join |
| Pseudonymise at the Silver boundary, key held in a separate store | One controlled point where identity is removed and one place where it can be restored | chosen |
| No dedicated zone, rely on column-level access control | Fewer moving parts | A single misconfigured role exposes Article 9 data; audit of who could see what becomes a query over role history |

## Decision

Personal health information enters Silver only through a pseudonymisation step. The re-identification key lives in a separate store (`keyring.duckdb` here) with its own access log. Gold never holds direct identifiers.

## Consequences

Positive: a data protection impact assessment describes one boundary; regulatory packs are produced from Gold without touching identifiers; partner data sharing operates on pseudonymised data by construction. Negative: any analysis that needs re-identification runs through a documented request; the key store is a single critical component with its own backup and recovery plan.
